====================================================================
2. NON-NEGOTIABLE GUARDRAILS
====================================================================

Science and data:

- The browser displays backend trend/evidence/forecast values exactly. It must never recalculate p-values, slopes, significance, confidence intervals or forecast eligibility.
- NASA POWER historical/reanalysis, Meteomatics operational weather and the internal monthly forecast remain distinct. Never average or silently blend sources/timescales.
- The coarse whole-Earth view is an explicitly labelled approximate orientation layer. It must not show exact values, p-values, forecasts or local scientific claims.
- Detailed local values, tooltips, calculations and significance always use real returned backend cells, even if the visual presentation is smoothed.
- News is context, not scientific evidence or an official warning. Only verified stories use green, amber or red attention markers; unverified news remains neutral/dashed.
- Why might this be happening is manual, source-cited, cautious and never a proof of causation.

Privacy and security:

- Keep the local-demo profile only. Discard sign-in passwords immediately; never send, log, hash or store them.
- No key, password, token, secret, environment-file content or provider response body may reach browser code, logs, tests, screenshots, Git or documentation.
- No Google imagery, Google tiles or Google branding. Any static Earth imagery must be licensed, credited, documented and never portrayed as live.

Accessibility and honesty:

- Maintain keyboard globe/timeline access, visible focus, 200% zoom support, accessible equivalent data tables/details and reduced motion.
- A smooth visual field may improve readability, but must be identified as a visual interpolation. It must not manufacture a measurement or conceal missing data.
- Do not fake a passing state or use placeholder data just to create a good screenshot.

3. REMAINING IMPLEMENTATION WORK
====================================================================

3.1 Earth visual finish — highest visual priority

Current issue: normal globe rendering still draws Natural Earth country-border curves. These are likely the unwanted lines the owner sees. The Earth must look smooth and natural, not like segmented map polygons.

Implement and verify:

- Remove country-border/graticule lines from the normal Main and Welcome Earth. Keep only a subtle, smooth land/ocean boundary if needed for legibility.
- Use one carefully calibrated natural Earth palette: deep but realistic ocean blue, restrained green/brown land, white ice/cloud, soft directional lighting and a thin physical blue atmosphere at the limb.
- Smooth coastlines, land edges and the outer limb through appropriate canvas/image resolution, anti-aliasing and draw order. No faceted seams, harsh curves, glowing outlines, map-slice appearance, CRT, radar or retro HUD.
- If a technical map needs country boundaries or graticules, expose them only in a deliberate Explore technical view; they are off by default.
- Preserve the black-space surrounding field and sparse stars, but never let decorative stars, grids, glows or particles compete with the Earth.

Acceptance proof:

- Capture screenshots of the Earth at several longitudes, zoom levels and display sizes. Review specifically for country borders, graticules, jagged coastlines, seams, inconsistent black shades and over-bright atmosphere.

3.2 Strong, smooth and honest data layers

Current issue: detailed selected-place data is still rendered as individual square cells, including tile outlines and an outer grid boundary. The coarse/local heatmap may still look too faint in an actual browser.

Implement and verify:

- Make temperature, rainfall and cloud-cover patterns visibly readable at normal viewing distance. Calibrate opacity/contrast/saturation in the real browser; the physical Earth remains visible below and a compact legend explains the scale.
- When the user chooses a metric, keep the instant labelled coarse global preview. It must appear without a point selection or expensive global point-by-point request.
- When the user selects/saves a place, replace or overlay its area with a smooth, surface-bound local field. Do not show square data tiles, tile outlines or a grid frame on the normal globe.
- Build the local field only from returned local grid values. Use a documented canvas interpolation/rendering method with a feathered geographic edge and correct globe clipping at the limb.
- Do not modify stored cell values or calculations. Exact values, missing values, provenance and the accessible data table remain available in Details. Missing data must not be blended into a plausible value; render an honest neutral missing area/pattern.
- Data layers must curve with the sphere, remain clipped to the visible hemisphere, cross-fade smoothly between real time frames, and never appear as a flat rectangular sticker.
- Maintain explicit observed/forecast separation through label, pattern and divider. Meteomatics stays a point/current-weather product rather than a historical/global field.

Acceptance proof:

- Screenshots and a short screen recording of each primary metric at world scale, a selected place, a month change, a forecast boundary and a missing-data example.
- Verify that the legend is understandable without technical language and that the detailed local visual is described as smoothed/visualised, not additional measured cells.

3.3 Real two-place comparison

Current status: code sends distinct latitude/longitude pairs, but this lacks final dedicated UI regression coverage and browser proof.

Implement and verify:

- Trace A and B state from picker, route/state, request payload, response and visual result. Fix any place where B can silently become A or where a stale result is reused.
- Display both place names and coordinates, and both values/series, clearly. Use line style, position, labels and symbols in addition to any data colour; do not rely on colour alone.
- Handle one unavailable place honestly: show which is unavailable while retaining the other valid result. Never replace a two-place view with duplicate values.
- Add frontend regression tests for distinct locations, A/B reversal, same-location handling and one-location-unavailable states. Keep the existing backend coverage.
- Browser-test a real two-place comparison end to end with deliberately different places and confirm the request/result identities.

3.4 Automatic news callout cycle while Earth rotates

Current issue: current globe logic can select up to three visible automatic callouts. The owner wants an elegant one-at-a-time popup sequence while the Earth spins.

Implement and verify:

- After saved-location news is available, maintain a deterministic, bounded queue of eligible saved locations currently on the visible hemisphere.
- Display only one autonomous popup at a time. Sequence: marker appears at its actual point; a fine leader grows outward along the sphere normal; the compact black/white callout fades/translates into place at the leader end.
- Hold briefly, fade it away, then select the next eligible visible location. Use a calm dwell time and do not repeat the same point until others have been considered.
- Recompute marker, leader and callout projection every animation frame. As a point reaches the limb or rotates behind Earth, tuck/fade the callout rather than letting it drift, overlap the globe or point to the wrong location.
- Do not stop normal Earth rotation merely because an autonomous callout is visible.
- Hover, keyboard focus, click, timeline scrubbing, dragging, an open drawer or a close/escape action overrides the automatic cycle immediately. Resume only after the relevant short idle state. Reduced motion disables autonomous cycling.
- Verified attention uses green/amber/red outline/glyph/leader only. The callout fill and main interface remain black/white. Unverified items stay neutral/dashed and include their label.
- The callout itself contains no more than headline, publisher, date and severity glyph. Open full details only on user action.

Acceptance proof:

- Record at least one full automatic cycle during normal rotation, a limb exit, a user hover/click takeover and a reduced-motion state.
