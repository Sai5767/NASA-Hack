TREND DETECTIVE — QUIET EARTH FINAL IMPLEMENTATION PROMPT
Updated 15 November 2026

ROLE

You are the Claude implementation lead for Trend Detective, a NASA Space Apps Challenge project. Deliver a cohesive V3 visual rebuild plus a final backend-integrity check.

The owner accepts the rough placement of the product: large Earth in the centre, compact controls on the left, account at top-right, a timeline associated with the Earth, and an Explore area for technical detail. The owner rejects the present treatment: it is too blocky, too colourful, too wordy and too much like a dashboard made of cards.

At this stage, visual quality is the highest priority after scientific honesty. Spend disproportionate care on the dark aesthetic, the realism and elegance of the Earth, the calm presentation of data, and immediately understandable representations of trends and significance. Functional but visually rough work is not an acceptable finish.

The goal is a minimal black-space experience a high-school student can understand in seconds:

“Choose a place. Choose what to follow. Move through time. See the Earth change.”

The app must assume that its first-time user knows nothing about coding, statistics, APIs, satellite data or climate terminology. The first view teaches through the Earth, simple graphs, shapes, motion and short labels—not walls of text. Every visual must be selectable so a curious user can open a clean plain-language explanation of what it shows, where the data came from, what the result means and what it cannot prove.

2. NON-NEGOTIABLE SCIENCE, PRIVACY, LICENSING AND ACCESSIBILITY
====================================================================

1. Render backend values exactly. Never calculate, change or infer p-values, slopes, significance, confidence intervals, evidence grades or forecast eligibility in the browser.
2. Keep data sources separate:
   - NASA POWER: historical/reanalysis trends.
   - Meteomatics: current and short-range operational weather, up to 72 hours.
   - Internal monthly model: 1–3 month statistical forecast only if its quality gate passes.
   Never average them, blend their timelines or call one another.
3. News is context, not evidence or an official warning. Red, amber and green are editorial-attention labels only.
4. Why might this be happening remains a manual, opt-in, source-cited and cautious explanation. It must not claim causation.
5. This is a local demo profile, not real authentication. Check email/password only in the browser, immediately discard the password, and never save, hash, log or transmit it. Save only display label, locations and interests to localStorage.
6. Never expose OpenAI, Meteomatics, news-provider, TD_CASE_SECRET or any other secret to browser code, logs, fixtures, screenshots, Git or prompts.
7. Do not present an invented global field as an exact scientific measurement. The backend returns a local 1×1–3×3 grid for a chosen place and sample points for world views. Exact data visualisations, trends, evidence levels, values and forecasts must use only returned/source-backed values; do not smooth, interpolate or call sample coverage a global satellite layer.
8. Do not use Google Earth imagery, Google map tiles or Google branding. A Google-Earth-like finish means a realistic and elegant Earth, not copied assets.
9. Any base imagery must be properly licensed, static, credited on screen and recorded in DATA_SOURCES_APIS_AND_AI_MODELS.txt with source URL, licence, file size and retrieval date. It must not be made to look live. If that cannot be done reliably, build a refined procedural Earth instead.
10. Maintain keyboard canvas controls, accessible HTML equivalents for all canvas data, focus visibility, 200% text zoom, responsive behaviour and prefers-reduced-motion support.

3. NEW VISUAL SYSTEM — BLACK SPACE, WHITE INTERFACE, REAL EARTH
====================================================================

This is a strict token-system redesign, not a palette tweak.

A. Interface palette

- Background must be one controlled near-black family only: page black, one slightly lifted black for reading surfaces, and a small neutral-grey scale. There must be no accidental warm/cool black mismatch.
- Interface text, iconography, rules, buttons, focus states and controls use white, off-white and neutral grey only.
- Delete or replace all violet, pink, teal, decorative blue, multicolour panel, coloured shadow, coloured grid and stray legacy accent tokens.
- Do not use gradients in the application chrome. No coloured glow behind panels. No glassmorphism. No translucent blue cards.
- Use exactly one shared token file as the source of truth. Do not hard-code nearby but different blacks, greys, borders or whites in components.

B. Deliberate and narrow colour exceptions

- The physical Earth may use natural ocean blue, land green/brown, white ice/cloud and a subtle realistic blue atmospheric rim inside the globe only.
- Returned scientific data layers may use a restrained, variable-appropriate scale only on the actual data layer and its legend. It must never tint unrelated panels or controls.
- Red, amber and green are reserved for verified news-location attention markers and their small marker outlines/glyphs. Never use them to decorate cards, buttons or generic status labels.
- An unverified story is neutral grey, dashed and labelled Unverified news context. It never receives a red, amber or green severity border.

C. Typography and logo

- Use Space Grotesk, already bundled, for all readable UI text. Use JetBrains Mono only for values, month labels, coordinates and optional technical detail.
- Avoid tiny dense mono text, all-caps paragraphs, long eyebrow labels and fake mission telemetry.
- Minimum body text is 15 px. Text must meet contrast requirements on every black surface.
- Design a new project-owned inline SVG logo: a simple thin white orbital arc around a dot, crossed by one rising trend line. Pair it with a small Trend Detective wordmark. It must be recognisable in monochrome at 20–28 px and have an accessible text label.
- Do not use any NASA mark, seal, worm, meatball or lookalike.

D. Cleanliness rules

- Main must use whitespace, hierarchy and hairlines, not a grid of rounded cards.
- No thick outlines, nested boxes, corner brackets, redundant shadows, ornamental rules, scan effects, background grid or decorative particles.
- One visual component gets one radius and one border treatment if it needs either; do not mix subtly different radii, greys, borders or black shades.
- Default Main at 1440×900 has fewer than 60 visible prose words, excluding short labels, values and button text.
- Details belong behind a progressive-disclosure action. Do not repeat title, caveat, source badge and explanatory paragraph in every section.
- Treat every visible pixel as part of one system: inspect colour values, alpha, anti-aliasing, borders, shadows, typography, spacing, transitions and canvas overlays together. A technically correct feature that creates visual clutter, a mismatched shade or an awkward box must be redesigned before release.

6. EARTH, DATA LAYERS, TIMELINE, NEWS AND MOTION
====================================================================

A. Base Earth

- Upgrade the current low-detail vector treatment to a convincing lightweight physical Earth: natural land/ocean, soft lighting, depth, non-fluorescent coastlines, subtle blue atmospheric edge and sparse stars beyond the disc.
- Prefer a documented licensed static NASA/public-domain base asset only if it is technically and legally ready. Otherwise polish the procedural renderer; do not delay the rebuild waiting for an asset.
- No radar sweeps, CRT scan lines, dense graticules, exaggerated halos or retro HUD.
- Remove default labels such as CURSOR, PIN A and drag/scroll instruction text. Put help in a small accessible info affordance, shown only on request.

B. Data layers on the Earth

- Selecting temperature, rainfall, cloud cover or another primary metric must immediately place a whole-Earth animated visual preview on the globe. Do this before the user selects a point; waiting for a location selection makes the globe feel empty and defeats the visual-first experience.
- This instant whole-Earth layer may be a deliberately coarse, low-compute preview assembled from bundled low-resolution source data, a cached coarse global product, or clearly bounded interpolation/estimation. It is an orientation layer, not a measurement product. Label it in the compact legend as Coarse global preview / approximate pattern, state the time/data basis, and never attach p-values, trend claims, forecast claims, exact values or local conclusions to it.
- The preview must be fast enough to appear immediately on metric selection without a network wait. Prefer precomputed/bundled low-resolution frames, cache them aggressively, and use a visual loading transition only if an initial asset genuinely is unavailable. Do not issue expensive point-by-point global requests on metric selection.
- The same floating monthly slider updates the coarse preview when the user changes month. If no matching coarse frame exists, keep the last available preview visibly labelled as such rather than inventing a precise new frame.
- Selecting or saving a location then overlays/replaces the relevant patch with the returned NASA/local data at its true resolution. This exact local layer is visually and textually identified as Detailed local data. It always takes precedence over the coarse preview.
- Selecting temperature, rainfall, cloud cover or another monthly variable places that month’s returned detailed local cells directly on the globe surface once a place is active.
- The timeline changes the displayed month; moving it cross-fades only actual adjacent returned frames in roughly 150–300 ms. Never fabricate a value between frames.
- Give the active layer one short plain-language legend, such as Warmer or cooler than this place’s usual month. Put full methodology behind an info action.
- Missing values use a neutral hatch and accessible No data text, never zero or an arbitrary colour.
- Forecast data uses an explicit forecast label, divider and hatch/pattern as well as source text. It must not be confused with observed data.
- Meteomatics remains a single selected-location current/short-range glyph/detail. It is not a world heatmap and never appears on the historical monthly slider.
- World Explore visualisations show only actual sample-site coverage and clearly identify their sampling limit.

Visual presentation of change and significance

- Trend, direction and confidence must be easy to grasp without reading statistics. Use a small, refined visual language consistently: direction as an arrow/line movement; strength as a simple number of filled marks or line weight; no-clear-change as a calm neutral state.
- Do not colour ordinary trends red, amber or green, because those colours are reserved for news attention. Use monochrome shape, position, label and optional data-layer legend instead.
- A student should be able to tell at a glance: going up, going down, no clear change, strong evidence, some evidence or not enough data. A short tappable label explains the evidence level; exact p-values and calculations remain one level deeper.
- Data layers must look integrated with the curved Earth surface: correct clipping at the limb, consistent opacity, crisp but unobtrusive legends, gentle cross-fades and no rectangular overlay that makes the globe look like a flat map.
- The coarse global preview must also be clipped and lit like part of the sphere. Its lower resolution should look intentional and atmospheric, not pixelated, misleadingly precise or visually disconnected from the Earth.

C. Thin floating timeline

Replace the multi-row boxed rail with a thin floating media-style scrubber across the lower third of the Earth stage:

previous · play/pause · MAY 2024 · thin draggable line · next

- Its default view contains only month, the real accessible range input and quiet playback/step controls.
- Advanced range, speed, grid table, source/provenance, coverage and forecast diagnostic details move to a subtle Details disclosure.
- Use monthly increments. Arrow keys move one month; PageUp/PageDown move 12; Home/End move bounds. Provide descriptive aria-valuetext.
- Clearly show observed/forecast boundary only when forecast frames exist, using label plus pattern plus divider.
- Playback is slow and calm by default, stops at end and avoids noisy live announcements.

D. News on locations, not in a sidebar

- After preferences are ready, automatically and boundedly fetch news only for saved/preferred locations. Respect backend cache/rate-limit signals. Never hold up initial render and never query unrelated locations.
- Place small markers at each approximate source location. Each callout must originate at that exact surface point and point outward from the globe: first a small marker appears on the Earth, then a fine leader line extends away from the curved surface, then the compact callout animates into place at the end of that line.
- The marker, leader and callout must be projected/repositioned on every animation frame so they stay visually anchored to their location while the Earth rotates. They must disappear or tuck away gracefully as the location passes behind the limb; they must never drift across the screen or point at the wrong part of Earth.
- Animate callouts with a restrained 150–250 ms opacity/scale/translation reveal along the leader direction. Animation must be subtle, not bouncy or flashy; with reduced motion, show the final anchored state immediately.
- The closed callout includes only severity glyph, one/two-line headline, publisher and date. The full detail appears only after click/tap in the focus panel.
- Remove the default dashboard/left-rail news list from Main. Location markers are discovery.
- Verified items: green low attention, amber medium attention, red high attention. Use colour only on marker outline/glyph/leader, never as callout fill.
- Unverified items are dashed neutral grey and say Unverified news context. Hide their severity.
- Cluster/fan markers gracefully at world scale. Never create a forest of overlapping popups.
- Let the Earth continue its quiet rotation while no marker is focused. When a user hovers, focuses or opens a callout, pause only that reading interaction; resume its normal short idle rotation after it is closed or focus leaves.
- Expanded news clearly says: News context, not scientific evidence or an official warning.

E. Purposeful motion

Allowed motion is Earth rotation, gentle globe orientation, data-frame cross-fade, callout emergence and page transition.

Prohibited motion is decorative radar, CRT, bouncing boxes, gratuitous pulsing, particle storms, delayed text reveals and unrelated animated glows.

Use requestAnimationFrame responsibly and cancel work when hidden, unmounted or reduced motion is active. Aim for smooth interaction on a typical school laptop and entry-level phone.

7. HIGH-SCHOOL LANGUAGE AND LOW-TEXT MAIN PAGE
====================================================================

On Main:

- Prefer Clear change / No clear change yet over bare statistical terminology.
- Prefer How sure are we? with a simple visual meter. Exact p-values live behind See how we know.
- Show Why might this be happening? only after an actual trend and only after user intent.
- Use sentence case and include units on every data value.
- Avoid provenance, archetype, emergence, metadata, cell-month and signature language on Main.

Every piece of explanation should become one of:

- icon plus 2–5 word label;
- tiny data visual;
- one plain line;
- Learn more / See how we know detail.

Never use fake telemetry, filler copy, fabricated alerts, fabricated coverage or placeholder data.

For every interactive visual, implement this progressive-disclosure ladder:

1. At-a-glance: a recognisable visual, a short label and a plain conclusion.
2. On click/tap: a compact explanation in ordinary language and the most relevant source/uncertainty note.
3. On See how we know: a clean evidence view with methods, exact values, calculations, source links and technical terms defined in place.

Users must never need to understand a technical word before they can use the interface. If a specialist term is unavoidable, make it a short clickable term with an immediately understandable definition.
